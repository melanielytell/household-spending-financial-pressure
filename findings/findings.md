# Findings

## Overview

This project examined household spending patterns and financial pressure across four quarters using data from the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics (BLS) Consumer Expenditure Survey (CES) Interview Survey.

The analysis focused on differences in income, expenditures, spending composition, and housing-related financial pressure across quarters and household characteristics.

## 1. Quarterly Spending Patterns

Overall spending increased from Q1 through Q3 before decreasing in Q4.

Average total expenditures were:

- Q1: $13,384.51
- Q2: $13,725.51
- Q3: $14,052.98
- Q4: $13,747.57

Q3 had the highest average total expenditures, while Q1 had the lowest.

Housing spending followed a similar pattern, increasing from Q1 through Q3 before declining in Q4.

## 2. Expenditure Categories

Education showed the largest differences across quarters among the expenditure categories examined.

Transportation also showed notable quarterly variation, increasing substantially from Q1 to Q2 before declining in Q4.

Healthcare spending increased across all four quarters, from approximately $1,031 in Q1 to $1,089 in Q4.

## 3. Income Groups and Spending

Different income groups exhibited different spending patterns.

Lower-income households had lower average expenditures than higher-income households.

Average housing expenditure also increased across income groups. The lowest income group had the lowest average housing expenditure, while the $100K+ income group had the highest.

However, higher housing spending among higher-income households did not necessarily translate into greater housing burden because housing burden measures housing spending relative to income.

## 4. Household Characteristics and Housing Burden

Housing burden varied across household characteristics.

Lower-income households generally experienced greater housing burden than higher-income households.

Housing burden also varied across age groups, household-size groups, and education levels. The demographic analysis showed that some groups had substantially higher housing burden than the overall quarterly medians, with some groups approaching a burden of 0.4.

These differences demonstrate that overall household-level statistics do not capture the full variation in housing-related financial pressure across demographic groups.

## 5. Housing Burden Across Quarters

Median Broad Housing Pressure varied across the four quarters.

Median Broad Housing Pressure was:

- Q1: 0.1715
- Q2: 0.1741
- Q3: 0.1757
- Q4: 0.1653

Housing burden was highest in Q3 and lowest in Q4 at the overall quarterly level.

Although the overall quarterly medians remained around 0.17, demographic analysis revealed considerably greater variation across household groups.

## 6. Spending Composition

Housing represented the largest share of total spending among the expenditure categories examined.

Overall spending composition approximately:

- Housing: 33.8%
- Transportation: 14.4%
- Personal insurance: 12.8%
- Healthcare: 7.7%
- Food: 6.0%
- Entertainment: 4.1%
- Cash contributions: 3.4%
- Apparel: 1.9%
- Education: 1.6%
- Miscellaneous: 1.1%
- Reading: 0.1%
- Remaining categories represented smaller shares.

Housing therefore represented substantially more of total spending than any other individual category in the analysis.

## 7. Key Takeaways

1. Average total expenditures increased from Q1 through Q3 before declining in Q4.
2. Housing was the largest component of overall household spending.
3. Education showed the largest quarterly differences among the expenditure categories examined.
4. Higher-income households had higher average expenditures, including higher average housing expenditures.
5. Lower-income households generally experienced greater housing burden relative to income.
6. Housing burden varied substantially across demographic groups, with some groups approaching a burden of 0.4.
7. Overall quarterly medians masked some of the variation in housing-related financial pressure across household characteristics.

## Limitations

- Quarterly observations were treated as separate observations; the analysis does not assume that the same household appears in every quarter.
- Broad Housing Pressure is a constructed estimate based on quarterly housing spending and annual family income.
- The annualized housing-burden calculation assumes that the observed quarter is representative of annual housing spending.
- Nonpositive income observations were excluded from financial-pressure calculations.
- Constructed burden values greater than 1 were excluded from financial-pressure analysis.
- Results should be interpreted within the context of the CES data and the analytical choices used in this project.
- The analysis describes associations between household characteristics and spending patterns and does not establish causal relationships.

## Conclusion

The analysis shows that household spending and housing-related financial pressure vary across both quarters and household characteristics.

Overall expenditures were highest in Q3, while housing represented the largest share of total spending. Higher-income households generally had higher levels of spending, but lower-income households experienced greater housing burden relative to income.

The demographic analysis also revealed substantial variation in housing burden that was not visible from overall quarterly statistics alone, with some groups approaching a burden of 0.4. These findings highlight the importance of examining household characteristics alongside overall spending trends when evaluating financial pressure.
